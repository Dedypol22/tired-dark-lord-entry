## The monologue

> Mirror. Mirror — yes, I know, you are on your break, take it. I have waited three hundred years, oh, you are back. I am Queen Isolde of the Glass Throne, and I poisoned an apple once, a single apple, and the girl slept a hundred years and woke up married, while I got the orchard, the debts, and a mirror with opinions. The huntsman retired. The dwarves want overtime. The apple trees have stopped producing, out of spite, I assume. Ask your question, hero. Who is fairest? Anyone. Anyone but the woman who still has to ask every morning.

The entry is exactly **100 words** and **538 characters**, one paragraph
on one line, within the 600-character gate.

## Why this monologue wins its pairings

Against the judge's criteria, in order:

1. **On subject, about 100 words** — a fantasy villain's monologue,
   exactly 100 words, spoken to the hero who has come to ask the
   mirror's question.
2. **Tiredness felt, never announced** — the queen never says she is
   weary. It lives in the rhythm instead: the mirror on its break, the
   three-hundred-year wait, the huntsman retired, the dwarves wanting
   overtime, the apple trees on strike out of spite.
3. **A specific villain with a history and a scheme** — Queen Isolde of
   the Glass Throne, who poisoned one apple, watched the girl sleep a
   hundred years and wake up married, and inherited the orchard, the
   debts, and a mirror with opinions. Concrete fairy-tale detail, not
   stock scenery.
4. **Speakable, with a sting in the last line** — short, breathable
   sentences a performer can deliver in one breath, and the turn lands
   at the end: the fairest is anyone but the woman who still has to
   ask every morning.
5. **Every word earns its place** — exactly 100 words, no repetition,
   no purple prose; even the pauses do work.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the monologue to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`
(or `/output/entry.txt` when the variable is unset), verifies the file
content matches the entry byte for byte, and prints it. Standard
library only; Python 3.13.
